# Online:Siege Weapons

Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Siege_Weapons
Fetched: 2026-09-12
License: CC BY-SA, UESP

Siege Weapons are constructs used by players within Cyrodiil for the Alliance War, mainly for the purpose of attacking or defending keeps. They may be purchased from Siege Merchants and Quartermasters, either with player gold or Alliance Points. Siege weapons must be set up from the Quick Slot wheel, and may not be deployed on sloped ground or too close to another siege weapon. Once deployed, you may exit the weapon and allow another allied player to fire it, but it can only be packed up by the player that originally set it up. Attackers and defenders may only deploy up to 20 siege weapons each at any keep (Defensers get 25 with the keep's Lumbermill at level 1 and 30 at level 2). Enemy siege weapons may be targeted by friendly siege weapons (except rams), damaging the weapon and any nearby enemy players, and destroying the weapon when its toughness reaches zero.

Siege weapons are alliance-specific, and can only be used by their matching alliance. You can store them in your bank or guild bank and trade them to other characters in your account or other players, but only they may only be used by members of the same alliance.

With the exception of battering rams, all siege weapons are immobile once deployed. In order to relocate them, they must be stowed, which can only be done when they are loaded and ready to fire. Once you deploy a siege weapon, you are locked into operation of that weapon, and can perform no other actions (except chatting). In order to perform other actions, you must either stow the weapon (X button), or exit the weapon (Alt or Option), the latter of which will leave the weapon deployed, where it can be used by another friendly player.

When deployed, siege weapons slowly decay, losing toughness over time, even if not being fired. If a siege weapon is deployed and left unmanned, it will decay at a much faster rate, quickly falling apart and disappearing. Siege weapons may also be lit on fire by enemy players, causing them to take 4,000 flame damage every 2 seconds for 1 minute. Burning siege weapons can be extinguished, which causes you to throw a bucket of water on them, dousing the flames. Siege weapons that are at less than full toughness can also be repaired with repair kits.

The Cold Fire and Cold Stone variants can only be obtained by completing dolmens in Cyrodiil, or randomly from reward boxes during special PVP events such as Whitestrake's Mayhem.

## Weapon Types
There are six types of siege weapons:

- Ballistae are good in all situations. Ballistae have a shorter reload time than trebuchets, but have lower damage and toughness. Ballistae come in four varieties: standard (strong against structures), Fire (causes fire damage to enemies over time), Lightning (ensnares enemies), and Cold Fire (stronger than Fire).
- Battering Rams are only effective for destroying the doors and gates of enemy keeps. Additional players may assist in the operation of a ram by standing near its structure while it is deployed, each of whom will increase its damage and its movement speed. Once deployed, rams can only move very slowly, so they are best deployed close to the enemy's door. Three allied players are required for the ram to be active and its efficiency improves for each additional player for up to six players. Only one Battering Ram can be deployed per Alliance at a keep.
- Catapults are mostly effective against enemies, especially in chokepoints, and come in three varieties: Oil (ensnares enemies), Meatbag 24 (causes a ground area dealing disease damage and reducing healing taken), and Scattershot (causes a ground area dealing physical damage and increases enemies' damage taken by 20%). Catapults have a shorter reload time than ballistae and are 50% tougher. They have a shorter range than trebuchets but a similar high trajectory than can deliver missiles over some obstacles.
- Flaming Oils are very effective against everything and are used mostly against enemy battering rams and players at gate keeps, but they can only hit targets close to and directly below them.
- Trebuchets are best used to destroy the walls and doors of enemy keeps but they deal terrible damage against enemy siege weapons. They have higher toughness than ballistae and catapults, have a very high arcing trajectory that can drop missiles behind enemy walls or atop higher elevations and deal higher damage but take longer to reload. Trebuchets come in five varieties: Stone (strong against structures), Firepot (causes fire damage over time to enemies), Iceball (ensnares enemies), Cold Fire (causes higher fire damage over time to enemies) and Cold Stone (stronger than Stone).
- Lancers were introduced in Greymoor. These Dwemer-made weapons are variants of the Ballista and fire five low accucary shots per burst. They come in three elemental types and can only be obtained via the Antiquities system. The Lead for them can only be found in Gladiator's Rucksacks by completing a Conquest daily quest in Cyrodiil, rewarding one of the following items: Fire Lancer Construction Crate, Frost Lancer Construction Crate, Shock Lancer Construction Crate.

## PvE usage
In PvE, you do not place your own siege weapon. Instead they are directly available on the terrain and you cannot stow them, and when destroyed they do not disappear.
- In Hel Ra Citadel, enemies can use ballistae and pour Flaming Oil on your allies below. You must either kill the enemies or burn the siege weapons.
- In two instances, Ballistae are provided directly on the terrain for your usage in PvE:
- During The Sublime Brazier quest in the Dragonfire Cathedral, where you could use two Firebolt Ballistae against Dremora opponents.
- In the Writhing Fortress, where both "Repaired Fire Ballista" and "Repaired Cold Fire Ballista" can be used and need to be repaired by either gathering "Ballista Parts" from barrels or from a synergy left by defeated enemies. They deal 90,000 damage against structures and on average 3,4 million Flame Damage against enemies. Shooting on your feet or allied companions will deal moderate flame damage in an area (Cold Fire Ballista Bolt) then moderate flame damage over time (Burning) (Not the status effect). The ballistae also lose 50,000 Health per shot, which is half of their health. In particular, the bosses Guuffertarez and mostly Zysax can only be harmed by them.

## Notes
- The Sundering Swing skill obtained from wielding Volendrung can be used to damage structures without requiring the use of siege weapons.
- The Cold Fire Ballistae are called "Cold Harbor Ballista" in the death recap.
