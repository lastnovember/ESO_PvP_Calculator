Bak-Deelith Dosu-Jasuda is a Stone-Nest Argonian warrior who can be found with his friend Bak-Deelith Xankapa in Eastern Solstice.

## Dialogue
There are multiple conversations available with Bak-Deeliths Dosu-Jasuda and Xankapa, the one you get is random.

### Encounter One
When you approach them, you may hear them discussing methods of killing daedra:
:Bak-Deelith Xankapa: "Watchers? I go for the tentacles first. Naturally."
:Bak-Deelith Dosu-Jasuda: "And I for the eyes. I would think an eyeless Watcher more helpless than a limbless one."
:Bak-Deelith Xankapa: "Perhaps this mainlander knows something of slaying Daedra."

You can then talk with Dosu-Jasuda:
:"Greetings, mainlander. I thought I was discussing a mutual love of slaying Daedra with my friend Xankapa, but she has decided to turn it into a competition, as she loves to do. I would ask for your input, but I do not wish to encourage her."
:Why do you like killing daedra so much?/ And why do you like killing Daedra so much?
::"I wish I knew. The other day, I was so taken with killing a band of Banekin that I nearly went in the portal they emerged from just to find more to kill. Others among my tribe have felt the same urge. We do not know why. But I have a theory."
::What is your theory?
::"That we are drawn by the whispers of Itz-Teyeesh. Sithis, as you mainlanders would say. This Planemeld is a forced, unnatural act. A defiance of the primordial force of chaos. Perhaps we are part of the natural resistance to this coupling."
::Then why didn't you end up going in that portal?
::"I would have, mainlander, had it not closed just as I made the choice to do so. If there is a next time, perhaps I will be swifter. And I will hope Xankapa is with me when the time comes. Her warrior's boasts are exaggerated, but not unfounded."
:Who are you?
::"I am Dosu-Jasuda of the Stone-Nest. Xankapa and I titled ourselves Bak-deelith, which means War-sophist in your language. When not debating, whether on philosophy or other matters, we serve as wandering warriors allied between our two tribes."
::Is it unusual for Stone-Nest and Tide-Born to be allies?
::"I would not say so. Though I would be hard-pressed to find any who are as close as Xankapa and myself. The arguing leads people to think we are nearly enemies. But it is our favorite activity, and we agree when it matters most."

After you finish your conversation, they will begin talking again:
:Bak-Deelith Xankapa: "I meant to ask. Have you found the way to best kill a Daedroth?"
:Bak-Deelith Dosu-Jasuda: "We Stone-Nest learned the waddle controls their fire breath. Slit it with claws or dagger, and the fight is much easier."

### Encounter Two
They can also be encountering arguing on the side of the road, regarding the nature of Sithis:
:Bak-Deelith Dosu-Jasuda: "We do not agree. On this, my will is as set as stone."
:Bak-Deelith Xankapa: "Only a fool thinks stone mightier than the will of the sea. Itz-Teyeesh shall prove me right."

If you talk to Xankapa, she will tell you to talk to Dosu-Jasuda so you can get his thoughts and then give your opinion:
:"Xankapa does not quite understand me. I mentioned stone as a contrast to her figurative use of the sea. She speaks of Itz-Teyeesh as being chaotic like the sea, but one can swim against the current. And what is more constant than the tides?"
:So what do you think? / And what do you think about Itz-Teyeesh that conflicts with Xankapa's view?
::"Itz-Teyeesh is the great Unmaker. All of life's chaos and changes are but fleeting until we return to him. He is, inherently, nothing. Fighting his will is as futile as clawing through stone. What do you think, mainlander? Share your thoughts."
::I agree with Xankapa. Sithis is more chaos than emptiness.
:::"I wonder then if it is chaos that heralds the great unmaking. That we see the shape of Itz-Teyeesh as outlined by chaos. For how can one truly look at or understand nothingness? You've given me much to think about, mainlander. I thank you."
::I agree with you. Sithis is more emptiness than chaos.
:::"And such emptiness is nothing to be feared, for there is no fear in the great Unmaking. Such nothingness might compel us to embrace lives of variety and color, yes? To make our time here count. Thank you for your insight, mainlander."plain
::I don't agree with either of you. I think Sithis defies true understanding.
:::"I would not disagree with you. Even knowing this, we do our best to make sense of what we cannot hope to understand, don't we? Or perhaps it is not like this where you come from. Be well, mainlander."
:Who are you?
::"I am Dosu-Jasuda of the Stone-Nest. Xankapa and I titled ourselves Bak-deelith, which means War-sophist in your language. When not debating, whether on philosophy or other matters, we serve as wandering warriors allied between our two tribes."

### Encounter Three
:Bak-Deelith Xankapa: "I meant to pay this custom of yours a compliment. A votive of stonework shows dedication. Devotion."
:Bak-Deelith Dosu-Jasuda: "There is so much more to be said for the sand murals of your Tide-Born. A bond that says you will weather the changing tides together. Or do I not understand it as you do?"

Speaking with Dosu-Jasuda about this disagreement after Xankapa asks you to get his reasoning:
:"I should have known better than to expect that Xankapa might finally agree with me. I cannot tell if she truly prefers the commitment customs of my Stone-Nest people, or if she simply seeks to rankle me. As she is very fond of doing."
:What is the Stone-Nest custom of commitment? / And what is the Stone-Nest custom of commitment?
::"Traditionally, we exchange totems of smooth stone. Each partner carves meaning into the cylinders, imbuing them with symbols and memory. On our anniversaries, the totems are returned. And then we exchange new blank stones, and the cycle begins anew."
::And what is the Tide-Born custom?
::"The annual Tide-Born commitment ceremony, as Xankapa tells me, involves partners finding a secluded stretch of sand near low tide. They draw intricate patterns in the sand together, then stay to watch the tide wash them away."
::Why does the Tide-Born tradition appeal to you more?
::"The sand mural speaks to me of Itz-Teyeesh. Recognition that our bonds, however many, however strong, are subject to the will of the great Unmaker. We must cherish and nourish our relationships not so they will last, but knowing that they won't."
:Who are you?
::"I am Dosu-Jasuda of the Stone-Nest. Xankapa and I titled ourselves Bak-deelith, which means War-sophist in your language. When not debating, whether on philosophy or other matters, we serve as wandering warriors allied between our two tribes."
::Is it unusual for Stone-Nest and Tide-Born to be allies?
::"I would not say so. Though I would be hard-pressed to find any who are as close as Xankapa and myself. The arguing leads people to think we are nearly enemies. But it is our favorite activity, and we agree when it matters most."

After you spoken with both, you can talk to Xankapa and tell her what commitment ceremony you prefer. Depending on your choice, the following will happen:
