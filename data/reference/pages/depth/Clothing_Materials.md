# Online:Clothing Materials

Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Clothing_Materials
License: CC BY-SA, UESP

There are four types of materials involved in Clothing: Raw Materials, Processed Materials, Tannins, and Gems.

Raw Materials are the materials that can be found in the world on plants or wild animals. By themselves they cannot be used, but they may be refined into Processed Materials at any clothing station. Unlocking the "Plentiful Harvest" and "Master Gatherer" perks in the Craft Champion tree will also provide bonuses when harvesting. There is a small chance of producing tannins when refining raw materials. The plants and medium armor materials you find are determined by your character's level and your Tailoring level-if you are level 40 with a Tailoring level of 2, you will find both Raw Spidersilk and Raw Flax out in the world.

Processed Materials are used to make Light and Medium Armor.

Tannins can be used to improve existing light or medium armor. Tannins can be obtained by deconstructing existing armor sets. Raw materials and tannins can both be acquired through Outfitter Hireling mails. Processed materials and tannins can be obtained as rewards for completing Clothier Writs.

Gems can be used to craft armor with traits, provided that trait has been researched on that piece. Gems can be found by looting jewelry boxes throughout the world, opening Wet Gunny Sack obtained while fishing, and deconstructing armor pieces that have traits.

## Tannins

colspan=2|Tannin | Effect

Hemming
Improve quality from Normal to Fine

Embroidery
Improve quality from Fine to Superior

Elegant Lining
Improve quality from Superior to Epic

Dreugh Wax
Improve quality from Epic to Legendary

In addition to clothing items, tannins are also used in Patterns for furnishings. Specifically:
- Normal quality patterns do not require any tannins
- Fine quality patterns require 9 Hemming
- Superior quality patterns require 6 Embroidery
- Epic quality patterns require 3 Elegant Lining
- Legendary quality patterns require 1 Dreugh Wax
