# Online:Armor

Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Armor
License: CC BY-SA, UESP

Armor comes in many shapes and sizes. There are three types of armor available: light, medium, and heavy. Light armor is geared toward mages, medium armor is geared toward stamina users, and heavy armor (the most protective type of armor) is geared toward frontline warriors. There are no limits to what armor you can wear; you can wear any armor or equip any weapon, regardless of race, class, or alliance.

In a switch back to the older gameplay of Oblivion and Morrowind, armor will degrade in combat and other circumstances (such as dying and respawning). There are repair kits you can carry around to make repairs in the field, or you can pay any merchant to do the work for you. Previously, the armor rating of a single piece of armor would gradually decrease as it degraded. As of patch 2.1.5, the armor rating stays the same until the piece of armor is fully damaged, after which its rating will be zero until repaired.

Armor is split into seven body sections, with different types for light, medium, and heavy armors: hats, helmets, and helms for the head; epaulets, arm cops, and pauldrons for the shoulders; robes (or jerkins), jacks, and cuirasses for the torso; gloves, bracers, and gauntlets for the hands; sashes, belts, and girdles for the waist; breeches, guards, and greaves for the legs; and shoes, boots, and sabatons for the feet. In addition, there are four more equipment slots for a disguise/costume, two rings, and a necklace, as well as two hand slots. A shield can be equipped in one of the hands to add to the armor rating and allow the use of One Hand and Shield skills.

## Enchantments
The following enchantments (all additive) can be added to armor:
- Deni: Glyph of Stamina - Increases Maximum Stamina
- Hakeijo: Glyph of Prismatic Defense - Increases Maximum Health, Magicka, and Stamina
- Makko: Glyph of Magicka - Increases Maximum Magicka
- Oko: Glyph of Health - Increases Maximum Health
