# Developer Deep Dive—Update 49’s Ability Refreshes

Cleave deep into the work behind the Dragonknight and Two
 Handed skill line’s look and feel updates.
Blade & Fury Reimagined
Arriving as part of Season Zero and going live with Update
 49 on March 9, both the
Dragonknight
and Two Handed skill lines will enjoy big updates to their ability animations,
 visual effects, and audio. To get some insight into the work that went into
 these updates, we reached out to Lead VFX Arist Cambria Blaize, Senior Sound
 Designer Lucas Edwards, Principal VFX Arist Brian Hahn, and Senior Animator
 Vincent Wong.
Since this effort was no small task, the various members of
 the refresh team had clear goals in mind when kicking off the first of these reworks.
“The primary goal for this update was to revisit the look
 and feel of the skill lines and reduce the feeling of floatiness in combat in
 conjunction with
planned balance
 changes
from the Combat Team,” explains Blaize.
In addition, the team wanted to ensure that what makes the Dragonknight
 and Two Handed skill lines so unique was retained and reemphasized in their
 redesigns.
“When animating both the Dragonknight and Two Handed
 abilities, there was a conscious effort to lean into brute physicality,” says
 Wong. “We wanted to make them feel powerful and satisfying to use without
 straying too far from how they felt in the past.”
“[The class’s] defining elements are definitely the use of
 fire, magma, and physical elements,” explains Hahn. “Dragonknight skills are
 all very grounded, and the player is often affecting things physically rather
 than purely through magic.”

Magma Armor
“Each Dragonknight skill line has its own flavor of audio design,”
 says Edwards. “For Ardent Flame, we focused on the power of ‘pure fire’ and
 tried to give its various elements that texture and shape. For Draconic Power, we
 infused a lot of dragon vocal elements with the fire itself and tried to give its
 various attacks a visceral and animalistic feel. With Earthen Heart, we focused
 on volcanic textures like magma, rock, and ash.”
Classic Abilities, New Challenges
Both the Dragonknight and Two Handed skill lines (and their
 abilities) were first introduced at
ESO
’s launch 12 years ago, and the
 team has enjoyed some tech and process changes since their original inception,
 inevitably changing how the work is done.
“Over the years we have gotten a lot more tech that allows
 us to do more intricate things, but we've also had rising standards for how
 things look when they go out the door,” explains Blaize. “There is a lot more
 involvement from multiple art teams to make these abilities look special than
 in the past.”
“In some ways redesigning the animations is easier because
 now we have an established style and standard to work toward,” says Wong. “Not
 to mention, there are a ton of lessons we've all learned through the years that
 we can now apply to modern abilities.”

Berserker Strike
“Figuring out a workflow and a cadence that gelled with the
 team was probably the most difficult part of the process, and I use the word
 ‘difficult’ generously,” continues Wong. “Everyone was great at communicating
 what was working and what wasn't. Sometimes we'd have to go back and forth to
 decide what made sense lore-wise or for other reasons, but we always had the
 same mindset during these discussions. We wanted what was best for the player.”
Similarly to the ability animations and VFX, when it came to
 reworking the audio, it was challenging to work on elements that players have
 come to know and love after over a decade of use.
“The original Dragonknight sounds are iconic, but over time,
 newer and more modern sounds have made their way into the game,” says Edwards. “We
 wanted to carve out more complex, layered sounds that nod to the original
 design and themes while bringing it into the modern day. To do this, we focused
 on making the shape of the sounds more impactful and readable during gameplay
 and ensuring that the source material was unique in a way that maintains
 identity.”

Dragon Leap
“The Two Handed skill line is very similar,” continues
 Edwards. “The animation and visual refresh really helped to make it feel
 smoother and more powerful, so we wanted the audio to have some more detail,
 shape, and grit to help it feel dangerous and exciting. We also wanted to give
 each flavor of weapon its own feel, so we spent time putting together unique
 impact sounds for each weapon type. It was a lot of work, and it is something
 that didn't exist in the baseline kit.”
In addition to the work that needs to go into capturing and
 recreating the overall feel of a skill line, each unique ability brought its
 own challenges in its redesign, too.
“I think the most difficult ability for me was Molten
 Weapons,” says Hahn. “On the surface it's just swapping out the players weapon
 models as part of an ability, but on the backend, it's way more complicated, as
 we've never actually done that in a player ability scenario. We spent a ton of
 time hunting down some obscure bugs and coming up with solutions.”

Molten Weapons
“Dragon Blood was probably the most challenging for me,”
 says Blaize. “Getting all the pieces for that in time for PTS was tricky, and
 our first version wasn't well received, so we had to go back to the drawing
 board a little bit and make some quick changes.”
“Fire is deceptively difficult in its own right,” says
 Edwards. “How do you create color, texture, and depth with something that is,
 essentially, just noise? I spent a lot of time creating complex textures that
 blended together to give the fiery abilities their own sound. I didn't want them
 to feel static. I wanted them to feel powerful and alive.”

Core of Flame
Roaring Results
Redesigning such iconic and popular abilities for some of
 the game’s original skill lines is a daunting task, but the team had a lot of
 fun during the rebuild, too.
“I really enjoyed dreaming up new attack animations for the
 Two Handed sword,” says Wong. “We wanted it to feel powerful and heavy, but
 also very satisfying when you land hits. We drew inspiration from not only
 other TES /medieval games, but real-life sword fighting, as well as stylish
 action games like Devil May Cry. It was fun to find the right blend of visual
 distinction, practical movement, and most importantly...satisfying attacks.”
“I think the most fun ability to work on was Wing Buffet
 [previously Protective Scale],” says Hahn. “It's one of those abilities that
 really showcases the combined efforts of all the different art disciplines that
 have their hands on this refresh. We got a new concept, model, rig, animations,
 FX, and audio.”

Reverse Slash
The result is a series of fresh-feeling abilities that the
 team is justifiably proud of.
“I'm really happy with how Dragon Leap came out,” says
 Edwards. “It took a lot of time to nail down, but it brings me a smile every
 time I use it. I'll put Engulfing Dragonfire and Lava Whip down as runners up—I
 wanted those iconic skills to feel really satisfying, and I'm happy with the
 result.”
“The new Momentum is just so satisfying to cast, and we were
 able to make it cleaner visually than the old version,” says Hahn. “We've been
 doing a lot of experimentation to see where we can make persistent FX cleaner,
 and Momentum is the most successful example.”
“Searing Strike is also another one that is just very
 satisfying to use. I'm happy we were able to get the alternating FX/animation
 in and working. It makes the ability feel so much better to cast on repeat.”

Searing Strike
“I love Lava Whip and its morph animations,” says Wong.
 “Lava Whip was probably the first animation updated for the Dragonknight, and it
 set the tone for myself and the team. My first attempt was acceptable, but our
 animation director felt like it was too safe and could be pushed further. That
 conversation elevated my ideas and enabled us to explore how far we were
 willing to go in general.”
“After we landed on what the new cast animation was going to
 be, the combat design team, effects, and audio team really went to work,”
 continues Wong. “There was a lot of back and forth between us during the
 process, and after the dust settled, we all stepped back and looked at what we
 made together. I think at that moment we all knew we had something special.”
