# Diving into the Dragonknight Class PTS Updates

Tier: combat-news
Published: unknown

Your feedback has resulted in even more changes to the
 Dragonknight class. Check them out here.
As part of the ongoing
Class Refresh
 work
, Update 49 and Season Zero will feature a complete rework of the
Dragonknight
 Class
. These changes to
ESO
’s fiery class were first able to be
 experienced on the Public Test Server in January, and based on your feedback,
 we’ve made a host of additional changes and adjustments. Discover what tweaks
 we’ve made thanks to your notes with this new breakdown from the development
 team.
Hey All!
We’re back with another Dragonknight update,
 focused on answering some of the most common
 questions we’ve seen, highlighting where your feedback has already
 made a difference, and giving you a transparent look at what to expect moving
 forward. As always, thank you for taking the time to share your thoughts
 during the last few weeks of the U49 PTS. Your participation
 genuinely helps shape the way this class evolves.
Update Goals
With the last few weeks of PTS under our
 belts, our goals with this update are simple:
Address
 some of the biggest questions and feedback we’ve seen
Show
 you where your feedback has led to real changes
Share some insight
 on the constraints we face and what can and cannot happen during a PTS
 cycle
We want you to know what’s changing and
 how your input continues to influence the future of
 the Dragonknight class.
Before we get into the specifics
 of what’s changing for U49 based on the PTS
 feedback, we want to acknowledge that opinions and
 preferences vary tremendously over the course of any PTS, but
 especially this one. We’re always working to make sure combat in
ESO
feels good
 for everyone. This means when looking at the class as a whole and thinking
 through how it feels for veteran players, casual and returning
 players, and even people picking up DK for the first
 time.
Some mechanics, especially ones involving shorter-duration
 timers or animation-tight rotations like weaving, can be tough for inexperienced
 players. We’re continuing to refine abilities and skill interactions
 so that the class feels rewarding, regardless of your skill
 level.
We’re also taking performance into account as we work
 through the Class Refresh. Hardware differences, frame rates, and
 connection stability can all impact how combat feels in ways
 that aren’t directly tied to ability function. We are
 continually working to minimize that gap where possible.
What’s Improved
A slew of bug fixes and adjustments have been made since
 PTS1. You can see the full list below, but expect improvements across
 animations, behavior consistency, and ability responsiveness.

 on Dragon Leap ability and its morphs, as well as the Wing Buffet
 ability and its morphs. Dragon Leap and its morphs fixed in PTS Week
 2; Wing Buffet and its morphs were fixed in PTS 3.
Fixed several issues with the
 Engulfing Dragonfire ability, including (but not limited to) players
 being able to recast the ability before the prior cast finishes, stacking
 damage. Fixed PTS Week 2.
Various Dragonknight visual effect issues
 for class abilities, including Corrosive Armor,
 Engulfing Dragonfire, Obsidian Shield, Fragmented Shield, and Fire
 Keeper. Corrosive Armor and Engulfing Flames were fixed in PTS Week 3; the
 others were fixed in PTS Week 4.
The Dragonfire Breath ability was not
 leveling up at the correct rate. Fixed in PTS Week 3.
Fixed a bug with Earthspike Mantle on the PTS where the
 armor abruptly disappears instead of naturally fading out. Fixed in PTS Week 3.
Fixed a bug affecting the Fire Keeper morph
 of Hearthfire where rapidly activating this ability resulted in
 VFX issues and a separate issue where this ability and its
 morphs didn't update their target sorting each tick. Fixed in
 PTS Week 4.
Fixed an issue where Core of Flame and its morphs were missing
 the 15% bonus potency modifier to their damage to account for their delays. Fixed in PTS 3.
Fixed an issue with Disintegrating Dragonfire where
 Major Breach was not extended by Elf Bane. Fixed in PTS 3.

We've seen your feedback that
 the Dragonknight class's overall ability strength still
 felt lower than ideal after PTS Week 1. We made the
 following adjustments in PTS 3 and 4:
Ardent Flame
Lava Whip: This ability and the Flame Last morph now
 generate 5 stacks for 20s instead of allowing rapid recasts; damage increased
 40% to compensate. This should smooth out rotations and
 reduce ability uptime conflicts.
Flame Lash: Grants 14% damage done (7% against players) for 45s
 if all stacks of Power Lash are expended and you are a Dragonknight.
Molten Whip: Seething Fury grants 5% damage done (2% against
 players) per stack if you are a Dragonknight.
Dev Note: Damage is intentionally inflated short-term so DK can
 keep pace with current meta while long-term solutions develop.
Searing Strike: DoT damage per tick increased 50%, making it
 effectively 2× stronger overall, accounting for GCD loss.
Searing Claw: Bonus per tick reduced to 10% (from 24%) since the
 base ability is now stronger. Bonus is now baked into base damage for accuracy
 and performance.
Core of Flame: Fixed missing 15% potency modifier.
Soul of Flame: Fixed Stamina
 restore incorrectly scaling from missing Magicka.
Dragonknight Standard: Reduced this Ultimate
 and all of its morphs' cost to 200, down from 250.
 Reduced the base damage reduction to 7-10% based on rank, down from 9-12%.
 Reduced the base Weapon and Spell Damage to 270-300, down from 345-375.
Standard of Might: The same changes to values as the base morph.
 This morph now grants the caster 15% damage done, 15% reduced damage taken, and
 300 Weapon and Spell Damage while inside the area, rather than 475 Weapon and
 Spell Damage and 12% reduced damage taken.
Dev Note: We’re balancing this Ultimate
 by aiding uptime potential, reducing some supportive strength,
 and restoring more caster power to echo the original Standard feel. This
 will avoid a full revert and long-term Ardent Flame
 oversaturation, while buffing values for the time being.
Draconic Power
Dragonfire Breath: DoT damage per
 tick increased 50%.
Disintegrating Dragonfire: Fixed Major Breach not being
 extended by Elfbane.
Engulfing Dragonfire: Stacking retuned to ramp more
 consistently—now +5% damage each tick (up to 50%) regardless of hits, instead
 of +20% every other tick on hit (up to 80%).
Dev Note: Restrictions from prior iterations felt unintuitive, so
 this morph was toned down to remain a fun, context-strong alternative.
Dragon Leap: CC now depends on the target type. Players are knocked
 back 4m + stunned 2s; monsters are launched upward + stunned 3s, restoring
 impact in PvE.
Earthen Heart
Corrosive Armor (Ultimate): This ability has been updated
 several times throughout the PTS cycle based on community
 feedback.
Damage cap is currently 3% of Max Health on live
 servers.
Increased to 8% with PTS 1.
Based on community feedback, the damage cap was further
 increased to 12% of Max health during PTS 3, with the DOT only
 affecting monsters in an effort to improve PvP
 balance.
With PTS 4, this morph’s penetration once again only
 applies to direct damage attacks, and the limit is 6% of
 your Max Health. Damage done was increased to 12.5%
 and targets all enemies instead of only monsters.
Dev Note: Penetration changes from PTS
 1 were rolled back based on PvP feedback. Penalties have
 been eased while maintaining a sharper trade‑off. This
 change still offers a higher damage ceiling than live with reduced duration and
 survivability, but far less extreme than earlier PTS weeks.
Molten Armaments: Damage cooldown reduced to 1.5s, aligning with
 faster heavy attack builds and pairing better with Empower.
Blessing at the Peak (Passive): Now triggers from casting OR
 dealing damage with Earthen Heart abilities; now grants 5|10% Critical
 Damage.
Superheated Ward: The change here is to address
 feedback regarding its morph, Magma Fist.
Magma Fist: This morph now gains
 an additional effect when hitting a target at max stacks of Heat
 Shock, which increases the damage done of your next Magma Fist by 66%
 within 6 seconds, up to once every 6 seconds. Heat Shock now lasts for 7
 seconds, rather than 10, and increases damage taken by 66, up from 33
 (originally 65 pre-PTS).
Dev Note: We’re adjusting Heat Shock to more
 closely match its original duration and power, addressing veteran
 player concerns and restoring some upkeep
 mastery. We’re also adding caster-focused strength to better
 support Stamina DK playstyles.

Additional Feedback
Some Abilities Felt “Slower”
We heard a lot of feedback about certain abilities feeling
 like they had more of a delay than before.
 Specifically, we’ve noticed Incinerate, Dragonfire Breath,
 and Chains of Flame being highlighted as examples.
Here’s a closer look at what’s really
 happening in these specific cases:
These abilities are not actually slower from a gameplay-timing
 perspective. Mechanically speaking, some are even faster under
 the hood.
To address the previous feedback that combat felt
 “floaty” at times, these animations now have clearer “launch” frames,
 which add definition and weight.
There is now more delineation between certain character
 movements, visual effects, and audio effects—creating more punctuation
 through the ability to create improved flow and causality.
We can interpret this additional visual and
 audio information as slower, even though the mechanics and
 timing remain the same.
This added weight is intentional: the goal is to make DK
 combat feel more powerful, punchy, and readable without sacrificing much
 or any speed of gameplay.
Balance, Power Levels, and Long-Term Plans
Following our forum post during Week 2,
Addressing Dragonknight Power Level on U49 PTS
, we saw
 further feedback and requests for changes on some
 abilities. Some pain points can be addressed quickly; others
 require more comprehensive solutions that take longer. The
 changes we have implemented are tweaks based on the early PTS
 feedback and data we’ve collected as more of you have entered
 PTS. Some feedback also can’t be
 solved immediately without risking bigger imbalances. As we progress
 through the Class Refresh effort, we’ll continue
 to monitor where imbalances lie and adjust as
 needed.
We previously mentioned that we are working on
 a longer-term solution to address the gap between subclassing and
 pure classing. We are working on a bridge solution that begins as
 a stopgap but will become a permanent enhancement. It
 will evolve based on your feedback and the shifting meta. The
 long-term class plan is bigger than any single PTS cycle, and this approach
 will help us move toward that future step-by-step. We understand that it
 may be tumultuous at times, but as we begin to flesh this out, we hope you’ll see
 these improvements come into focus one piece at a
 time. We’ll have more information on this in the coming months as we
 begin to build it out.
PTS Cycle Limitations
One of the questions we frequently see when
 we roll out weekly PTS updates is “Why can’t X change be made
 instead?”  We try to act on as much of your feedback as we safely
 can, but there are limits to what can change from one week to the
 next.
What Can Change Week-to-Week
We can make small, safe adjustments, things
 like some number tweaks, or simple fixes
 that don’t ripple across the rest of the game. These are faster, lower
 risk changes that let us respond to your feedback quickly without
 destabilizing anything big.
What Can’t Change in an Incremental
 Update
Larger reworks for things like major system changes, ability
 redesigns, sweeping changes to animations/FX/audio, or anything that
 touches multiple interconnected
 systems aren’t typically achievable in a single PTS week,
 or sometimes an entire PTS cycle, depending on the scope.
 Those types of changes require deeper planning, testing, and
 coordination across several teams and more time invested.
We’d also like to allow enough time for
 feedback we receive to settle into meaningful data. This helps
 us to better understand whether an issue truly needs action, rather than
 overcorrecting. As discussions continue, our goal is to identify the
 recurring concerns so we can focus on changes that will have the
 greatest overall impact.
Why These Limits Exist
Each update needs to stay stable, testable, and aligned with
 the long-term vision for Combat and the Class Refresh. Changes that
 require the work and coordination of several internal teams can also cause
 other fixes or updates in a package to become delayed or result in not enough
 time to adequately test them due to shared resources. Iteration is good,
 but only when it doesn’t break or heavily delay something
 else in the process.
We know many of you have questions about the length of time
 outlined in the Combat roadmap and why certain changes take longer than others,
 and those questions are fair. We want to talk a bit more openly about
 timelines, especially around larger efforts like the Class Refresh because we
 know it’s something many of you are eager for and curious about.
When we say a project like this spans multiple Updates, or
 even years, it’s not because it’s sitting in a queue waiting its turn. It’s
 because of the scale and complexity involved in doing it well.
Class abilities aren’t contained within a single system. As
 we hope you've seen during our regular PTS Weekly Updates, these changes affect
 animations, sound effects, balance, progression, and how that class interacts
 with every other class and encounter across the game. Even small adjustments
 can ripple outward in ways that require careful coordination and testing. The
 bar for quality here is high, and it should be.
That said, we don’t want you to wait years before seeing or
 feeling progress. That’s why we’re breaking this work into phases with a
 multi-Update roadmap that allows us to approach these changes thoughtfully and
 sustainably. With this approach, improvements can reach you as they’re ready,
 and so your feedback can actively help shape what comes next. It's our hope
 that you're beginning to see this come together through the PTS process with
 the Dragonknight changes.
Our goal is to be transparent without overpromising,
 ambitious without cutting corners, and collaborative rather than distant.
 Sometimes fixes may come in the form of short-term adjustments and long-term
 solutions, and we appreciate your patience, your passion, and the fact that you
 care enough to ask these questions in the first place.
How You Can Help Provide Useful Feedback
Feedback that is focused on specific problems, rather than
 proposing various solutions, helps us to better identify the source
 of the issue. As we outlined above, sometimes what may seem like an
 easy fix isn’t necessarily the best option due
 to rippling effects or impacts in other
 areas. Problem-focused feedback helps us better determine what
 the best possible solutions are in both the short and long
 term.
We hope this gives you a clearer sense of how
 your feedback has helped shape these updates, as well as the
 constraints we’re balancing throughout the PTS cycle. With PTS 4 now
 live, we won’t be adding further incremental patches for the time
 being, but the server will stay open for anyone who wants to keep testing,
 exploring, or just playing around. Thank you again for
 everything you’ve shared with us so far, and as always, please keep
 the feedback coming.
